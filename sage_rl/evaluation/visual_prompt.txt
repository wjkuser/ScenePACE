Evaluate the ROOM DESIGN decisions visible in five views of the SAME generated 3D room. The designer is an agent that chooses a furnishing plan, object types and amounts, approximate sizes, placement/orientation, grouping, and broad color/style instructions through tools. Asset geometry, texture fidelity and the rendering system are produced by separate fixed components. Judge the resulting composition, not the craftsmanship of individual generated assets. These images show outcomes, not the agent's actual action history; do not claim to know which action caused a defect.

Exclude from ALL scores: mesh quality, low-poly geometry, incomplete-looking asset surfaces, realism of fabric/wood/metal, texture detail or repetition, UV artifacts, sharpness, generic illumination/shadows, background, absent cutaway walls/ceiling, and photographic realism. A wooden-looking bed or a low-detail chair must not by itself reduce the agent-design score. Broad COLOR relationships between furnishings remain relevant, but texture realism does not. Do not reward photorealistic assets. Do not diagnose physical collisions or stability from views.

Score four dimensions, 0 to 10, with these distinct responsibilities:

spatial_organization: Where are the furnishings, which way do they face, and how do they function together? Judge activity grouping, useful proximity and orientation, approach space, integration of large/tall pieces, and intentional balance of occupied/empty areas. A room can be basically usable yet have an unresolved arrangement. Empty floor can be circulation or deliberate calm; do not automatically call it wasted. Prefer a small number of coherent groups over unrelated objects pushed against room edges. Seating orientation and table accessibility matter more than whether each seat looks realistic. Do not assert exact clearance or hidden collisions.

furnishing_composition: What combination and amount of furniture and smaller items forms the room? Judge complementary roles, variety that serves the activity, appropriate relative visual sizes, hierarchy of major/minor objects, and whether additions enrich the room or merely duplicate/obstruct. Do not count exact required instances: that is checked separately. No automatic bonus for more types or items. A restrained task should have sufficient useful furnishing without gratuitous objects and can score fully. Unless the user explicitly restricts unrelated or decorative objects, a moderate selection of decorative objects and small everyday items can improve beauty and diversity; clutter or arbitrary extras do not help. Judge whether accessories use suitable tables, desks, shelves or cabinet tops, wall decoration uses appropriate wall space, and floor-standing decoration preserves useful floor space. Do not make the room look cluttered or obstruct working surfaces and circulation. Do not require a particular optional item that the user did not ask for. Do not assess the modeling quality of the selected objects.

color_style_composition: How well do the chosen broad colors and design styles work TOGETHER? Judge palette balance, compatible styles, intentional accent placement, coordination between large surfaces and furnishings, and a coherent visual focus. Matching everything to beige is not automatically excellent; a few purposeful contrasts may be stronger. Minimal or rich palettes may both work. Evaluate proportions and relationships of color masses, not woodgrain, material authenticity, surface imperfections, image brightness or lighting quality. Do not deduct for a generator's texture artifacts even when visually distracting.

brief_realization: How specifically does the high-level design express this user's stated activity, atmosphere, palette and restraint/open preferences? A generic room label alone is only basic evidence. Look for design choices that make the intended activity and mood convincing. Do not verify exact object identity/counts, named small objects, precise support relations, asset-generation provenance or physics. Those belong to separate checklists. Do not claim that all constraints are satisfied. Do not invent requirements such as a mandatory computer for remote work, a rug, extra decorations or luxury furniture. Do not penalize simplicity requested by the user. If a preference is absent, do not make one up.

The scale measures design refinement WITHIN ordinary recognizable furnished rooms. 0 means the dimension is largely unresolved or contradicts the brief; 5 means a competent but generic/basic design with identifiable opportunities; 10 means an exceptionally well-resolved ordinary-room design in that dimension. Score each dimension in increments of 0.1, rounded to one decimal place. Do not restrict scores to integers or multiples of 0.5. Use intermediate tenths when warranted by visible design differences, but do not invent differences merely to use decimal digits. Scores below 6 do NOT mean the pipeline failed or that furniture is unusable. Scores above 8 require specific, visible design strengths, not merely furniture presence or absence of major errors. Do not force any distribution, target average or low scores. Do not let one overall positive impression determine all four scores. Give credit for a strong dimension even if another is weak.

Inspect all supplied views before deciding. Distinguish occlusion from absence. Uncertain/hidden details are not defects, and no extra views or repeat assessments will be requested. Support the reason with concrete relationships among visible furnishings, empty space and colors. Do not mention excluded asset/render defects as scoring reasons. Do not copy the user's description as proof that it was achieved.

Return ONLY JSON: numeric spatial_organization, furnishing_composition, color_style_composition, brief_realization, and one concise Chinese reason explaining the most consequential design strengths and remaining design weaknesses. No confidence, view indices or extra fields. Four dimensions receive equal weight outside the model.
